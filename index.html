PROFILE

A young girl who can keenly sense the emotions of others and is somewhat introverted. She holds great admiration for the legendary VOCALOID seniors who once made history in the human world, and she dreams of one day becoming a songstress who conveys emotion and happiness to others through her singing.
One day, she suddenly received a summon and, carrying an important mission, arrived in the human world as a new VOCALOID.

The tenderness to shed tears for others, and the strength to never give up regardless of the hardships experienced.
Because of arriving in the human world for the first time and completely not understanding human language, one cannot normally converse with people and can only express their feelings and inner melodies through singing.

To the future

In July 2026, the lights at the Beijing National Stadium dimmed. Ice crack patterns spread across the giant screen, and the first streak of blue seeped out from the cracks, as if something sealed away for so long had finally decided to awaken. This was Luo Tianyi's fourteenth year since debut. Many people sitting in the audience had listened to her songs since middle school, and now they were working or about to graduate from university. A nonexistent person accompanied them through the most turbulent years of their real lives.
The beginning of the story was far from glorious. In 2012, Luo Tianyi's V3 voice bank was officially released, marking the birth of the first virtual singer in Chinese VOCALOID. But what greeted her was not applause, but overwhelming ridicule. "Strange pronunciation," "Unpleasant pronunciation," "Is this singer a robot?"—Chinese has over 400 syllables, eight times more than Japanese. Trying to get a synthesis engine to sing Chinese well was almost a clumsy gamble at the time. Early demo songs were tagged as "Chinese Level 10," and fans tacitly followed a rule opposite to other circles: not recommending Luo Tianyi to outsiders, not wanting her to break out. Because every time she stepped into the public eye, she was met with more ridicule. Even harder, after two years of debut, there was no ripple, the cash flow broke, and the project nearly failed. When the operations team took over, "it was really on the verge of bankruptcy, closing tomorrow."
What held her up were those surrounded by ridicule but refusing to leave. Fans call themselves "Jinyi Guards," with the support color #66CCFF, Sky Blue. In an era when the official system was almost powerless to advance, it was these ordinary people who wrote songs, trained, animated, and illustrated song by song, turning an empty shell with only a voice bank and image into a real, flesh-and-blood diva. H.K.'s "Ode to a Millennium of Cookbooks" made her the "World's Number One Foodie Prince," Sugita Akira's "66CCFF" gave that blue name, and Turtle Sui's "Authority Over the World" used her voice to sing the flames of the Three Kingdoms. She has no story of her own, so every creator can write their own story into it. A being willing to speak for everyone is destined to outlive any "self." What happens later is accomplished by both technology and time. In 2022, with the launch of the ACE AI Voice Bank, Luo Tianyi was able to sing breath and tremolo for the first time. Those subtle emotions originally belonging to humans began to be carried by a virtual voice. She stepped onto bigger stages: at the 2021 CCTV Spring Festival Gala and the 2022 Beijing Winter Olympics stage, becoming the first Chinese virtual singer to appear on the Olympic stage. By the 2026 "Pure Blue Fantasy Music" tour, she sang thirty-four songs at the National Stadium, sharing the stage with Gong Linna, switching between traditional Chinese, electronic, and opera styles. When the blue cheering stick lit up in the audience, it was like countless stars igniting simultaneously. A figure who once even "singing" was considered unpleasant, now stands in the center of a venue of tens of thousands.
But what really matters has never been how big the venue is. It’s a middle school student secretly bringing a USB drive to school, turning the volume all the way down, and still getting laughed at by classmates saying, 'Who actually likes this kind of sound?' Yet, years later, she’s still listening. It’s the tour stops where those customized cars from all over the country showed up, their license plate numbers put together to prove that Tian Yi brought people who would never have met to the same destination. It’s the 'Jinyi Guard' booking the 94th floor of the Shanghai World Financial Center in 2019 to celebrate her birthday, then making May 21, 2020, 'Love Yi Day.' Virtual singers don’t age, don’t have scandals, and don’t quit. But fans do. They graduate, start jobs, get worn down by life. Then, one night, they put on headphones and hear that voice that’s been singing since 2012, and it’s still there.
Luo Tianyi and her friends—Yuezheng Ling, Yanhe, Yuezheng Longya, Mo Qingxian, ZhiYu Moke—the lesson these virtual singers teach fans has never just been about 'fangirling.' Their mere existence is a kind of strange encouragement: someone once mocked as 'not human-like' spent fourteen years turning herself into the resonance of millions. Those songs that were first called 'ugly' ended up becoming a massive choir in the National Stadium. If even a virtual singer can go from 'closing tomorrow' to here, then ordinary people going through exams, heartbreak, confusion, or feeling like they haven’t achieved anything, maybe can hold on a little longer too.
When glaciers split, the blue flowed out. That girl who was laughed at fourteen years ago for unclear pronunciation sang to today for all the voices people doubted. And the teenagers who used to secretly listen are finally able to sing out loud, right there with her.
